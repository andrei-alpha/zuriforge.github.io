Hard Maple Faceted Diamond Board

A 28 cm × 24 cm geometric serving and cutting board sculpted from solid North American Hard Maple (Acer saccharum). Engineered with a custom dual-rail plunge routing jig to carve multi-angle brilliant-cut diamond facet grooves, hand-contoured along its gem silhouette, and inlaid with deep emerald green epoxy resin.

Project Specifications & Parameters

Dimensions 28.0 cm length × 24.0 cm width × 2.2 cm thickness
Construction Style Two-Panel Bookmatched Edge Glue-Up (Parallel clamp cauls, waterproof PVA matrix)
Wood Species North American Hard Maple / Sugar Maple (Acer saccharum), prime clear heartwood
Janka Hardness 1,450 lbf — Dense, closed pore structure, traditional butcher-block timber
Pattern & Geometry Brilliant-cut diamond gem silhouette with radiating crown, table, and pavilion facet vectors
Jig & Tooling Custom dual-rail wooden guide track with zero-tolerance center channel for DeWalt plunge router
Inlay Material Deep Emerald Green translucent casting epoxy resin with metallic mica depth
Perimeter Profile Faceted 7-sided gem silhouette with ergonomic underside chamfer
Surface Finish Food-grade USP mineral oil saturation & organic Swiss beeswax Holz-Butter polish
Food Safety 100% food-contact safe, zero VOC fully cured resin matrix, knife-friendly edge-grain surface

Workshop Journey

The Complete Build Log

A step-by-step documentation of timber dimensioning, guide jig engineering, multi-axis plunge routing, contour sculpting, and resin inlay curing.

Step 01 Lumber Preparation

Selecting & Dimensioning Prime Hard Maple

North American Hard Maple (Acer saccharum) is the undisputed gold standard for culinary cutting and serving boards. With a Janka hardness rating of 1,450 lbf, its fine, closed cellular structure resists moisture absorption, prevents bacterial infiltration, and holds razor-sharp router details without splintering. Two matching clear slabs were jointed square, planed to a uniform 22 mm thickness, and dimensioned on the workbench.

Two clear Hard Maple boards prepared for bookmatched edge lamination. Click to enlarge
Step 02 Panel Lamination

Edge Glue-Up & High-Pressure Bar Clamping

The jointed edges were dressed with waterproof PVA wood glue and assembled into a continuous 28 cm × 24 cm panel. Heavy-duty parallel bar clamps applied consistent lateral pressure across the joint line to ensure complete adhesive squeeze-out and a seamless, structural glue bond that will never separate under culinary washing.

Parallel clamp matrix ensuring uniform pressure and an invisible centerline joint. Click to enlarge
Step 03 Jig Engineering

Fabricating the Custom Dual-Rail Router Guide Jig

Freehand routing straight grooves across dense hardwood end-grain and side-grain introduces unavoidable wander, tear-out, and uneven channel depths. To achieve flawless, millimeter-accurate straight grooves along angled facet vectors, a custom dual-rail wooden guide track was fabricated with an exact center channel calibrated to capture the baseplate of the plunge router.

Dual-rail guide jig: Captive parallel rails constrain the router into true linear travel. Click to enlarge
Why a Dual-Rail Jig is Essential in Hard Maple

Hard Maple's dense fibers exert significant rotational kickback on router bits. Single-fence guides can deflect under feed pressure, creating wavy lines. A captive dual-rail jig completely eliminates lateral deflection, ensuring every facet line is dead-straight and uniform in width.

Step 04 Tool Calibration

Plunge Router Depth Calibration & Stop Collars

A DeWalt plunge router was fitted with a precision straight spiral carbide bit and mounted onto the guide rails. The turret depth stop was micro-calibrated to rout a crisp 4.0 mm channel depth across all passes, creating optimal volume for the resin inlay while retaining the structural integrity of the 22 mm solid maple panel.

Plunge router mounted in the guide track: Calibrated for uniform 4mm groove depth. Click to enlarge
Step 05 Geometric Layout

Drafting the Brilliant-Cut Diamond Facet Geometry

Using precision drafting squares, protractors, and fine-line layout markers, the classical geometry of a brilliant-cut gemstone was plotted directly onto the cured maple face. The layout maps out the horizontal table facet, angled crown facets, side pavilion vectors, and the bottom culet point, proportioned precisely to fit the 28 cm × 24 cm blank.

Mathematical gem layout: Facet vectors mapped across crown, table, and pavilion planes. Click to enlarge
Step 06 Scale Verification

Scale, Proportion & Ergonomic Hand Check

Before committing cutting tools to the wood, the physical scale of the diamond design was verified in the workshop. Measuring 28 cm across and 24 cm high, the proportions offer generous serving surface for artisanal cheeses, charcuterie, or appetizers, while remaining lightweight and balanced for one-handed serving.

Scale check: Ensuring generous culinary real estate within an elegant faceted silhouette. Click to enlarge
Step 07 Precision Routing

Multi-Pass Plunge Routing Along Vector Angles

For each individual facet line, the dual-rail jig was aligned with extreme precision and locked into place with heavy F-clamps. Routing was executed in progressive 2.0 mm depth increments at high spindle RPM to prevent heat buildup and wood scorching in the dense sugar maple fibers.

Jig alignment: Clamping rails along individual facet vectors for clean, scorch-free passes. Click to enlarge
Routing in progress: Crisp 4mm deep channels carved cleanly without splintering. Click to enlarge
Workshop Ethos Health & Safety

Respirator & Eye Protection Under Heavy Fine Dust

Hard Maple generates exceptionally fine, airborne cellulose dust during high-speed routing that can irritate the respiratory system. A 3M half-face particulate respirator mask with P100 filtration and high-impact safety goggles were worn throughout all machining and profiling stages.

Essential workshop safety: P100 respirator protection during dense hardwood machining. Click to enlarge
Step 08 Channel Architecture

Completed Facet Grooves & Perimeter Margin

With all 12 interior facet channels routed to depth, the continuous outer border channel was cut. The intersections of all angled vectors were refined with micro-chisels to ensure sharp, clean vertex junctures where resin will pool uniformly.

Completed routing: Geometric facet relief ready for edge contouring and resin casting. Click to enlarge
Step 09 Contour Sculpting

Faceted Silhouette Cutting & Perimeter Beveling

The maple blank was cut away following the seven outer faceted edges of the diamond gemstone. A smooth under-bevel was routed along the lower perimeter to create a floating visual effect on dining tables, giving the user an ergonomic natural grip when lifting the board.

Edge detailing: Smooth chamfered corners and clean vertex intersections inspected by hand. Click to enlarge
The sculpted diamond silhouette: 28cm × 24cm faceted board resting on the studio bench. Click to enlarge
Step 10 Resin Inlay & Cure

Deep Emerald Green Epoxy Inlay Casting

A two-part, low-viscosity food-safe epoxy resin was infused with metallic emerald green mica pigments, creating an alluring shimmer reminiscent of Colombian emeralds. The liquid resin was carefully hand-poured into every facet groove, slightly overfilling the channels to allow for surface tension and post-cure flush sanding. The board was left in a dust-free workshop enclosure to cure to full rock-hard shore hardness over 72 hours.
